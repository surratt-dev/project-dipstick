# Facilitator Review — session-timeout-continuity (Issue #133)

**Reviewer:** Priya Nair, Facilitator (Subject Matter Expert)
**Reviewing:** `exploration-notes.md` (Devon Calloway), against `connectionHealth.ts`, `ReauthRequiredTreatment.tsx`, `FacilitatorReadinessGrid.tsx`, the BRD (FR-4.5, FR-4.6, OR-1.1–OR-1.2), and the Live Voting use cases (disconnect/reconnect scenarios, lines ~280–330).

---

## Overall

Devon's read of the code matches what I found checking it myself: `connectionHealth.ts` really does treat the absolute-timeout close identically to a wifi blip today, and `FacilitatorReadinessGrid.tsx` confirms the `reauth-required` state fully supersedes the grid — no rows, no markers, nothing — the instant it fires. I agree with the recommendation: route the absolute-timeout force-close through `REAUTH_GRACE_EXPIRED_CLOSE_CODE` rather than inventing new continuity machinery. That's the right instinct and it's a real bug, not a missing feature.

But "the client lands in a known state instead of an infinite spinner" is a *correctness* bar, not a *usability* bar. From my chair — the person actually running the ritual — landing in `reauth-required` is progress over today, but the component as it stands today is not yet a good experience for the person it's about to start showing up for. Below is what I'd want Design to carry forward.

## 1. The shared reauth copy makes a factually wrong claim when shown to a facilitator

`ReauthRequiredTreatment.tsx`'s copy: *"Any vote you haven't submitted yet will be lost."*

I checked the BRD (FR-4.5, OR-1.2) — the facilitator never casts a vote and never sees vote content before reveal; the whole point of my role is to be uninvested. When this banner takes over *my* screen mid-session, it will be telling me something that isn't true about my own situation, and it says nothing about what actually is true: I'm about to lose my ability to trigger a reveal or advance the topic, and I don't know what state I'll come back to.

I understand why this is written the way it is — D9's role-blindness (no props derived from cause, role, or session-moment state) exists specifically to stop the banner from forking and drifting again, and I don't want to re-litigate that decision lightly. But I want Design to treat this as a live question rather than settled by inheritance: is a single, role-blind sentence still the right call now that one of the two call sites (the facilitator's) is a role for whom the sentence is untrue? A role-blind *mechanism* (one component, one close-code path, one state machine) doesn't require role-blind *copy* — those are separable, the same way I separated "shared component" from "shared ARIA role" in my review of the original banner. At minimum I'd want the vote-loss sentence gated so it doesn't render for the facilitator call site; whether that requires a prop (reopening D6/D7/D8's blindness bounds) or something coarser is Design's call, but shipping a knowingly false sentence to the one role who has the whole room waiting on them is worse than the drift risk D9 was guarding against.

## 2. What actually happens to the room while I'm gone isn't answered anywhere — including in the use cases

I went and read the disconnect/reconnect use case directly (not just Devon's citation of it). It explicitly puts **"Facilitator disconnection and reconnection"** and **"Notifying other participants of a disconnection event"** in its own **Out of Scope** section, with the one-line justification "the session cannot proceed without the facilitator." That's true as far as it goes, but it means there has never been a spec'd answer to the question this issue actually raises for me: what do participants see, moment to moment, while I'm on the other side of an OIDC redirect for however long that takes?

Walking through it concretely, as it would actually happen mid-topic: I've just told the room to lock in, people are voting, my absolute timer fires. Participant connections are unaffected — they keep voting into the room's backend state same as always, because nothing about their own session expired. What stops is only the one thing only I can do: trigger the reveal or advance the topic. To the room, this looks exactly like I went quiet — no banner, no indicator, nothing distinguishing "Priya is thinking" from "Priya's connection just died and she doesn't know it yet." If it resolves in the time it takes me to notice, click through login, and land back — call it under a minute — that reads as an ordinary pause. If the IdP redirect hangs, or I fat-finger the wrong account, or my laptop wakes up slowly, that silence stretches, and now I have a room of engineers looking at a screen that isn't moving, with no explanation, in the middle of a ritual that depends on feeling like a conversation, not software. That is a worse outcome than a clean, boring "facilitator reconnecting" line would produce, and it's *precisely* the "does the room stall" question I was asked to think about here: yes, functionally the room stalls the moment my timer fires, regardless of how gracefully my own client handles it — the open question is only how long, and how visible the cause is to the people waiting.

Devon's Open Question 5 leans toward "might be unnecessary." I'd push back on that lean, gently: the reasoning given is "participants can't do much without the facilitator regardless," which is true of *capability* but says nothing about *experience*. A quiet, one-line, non-alarming indicator ("Facilitator reconnecting…") costs very little and directly answers the moment I actually worry about — the awkward stretch where the room doesn't know if the tool broke. I'm not asking for anything gamified or a countdown; the restrained, `reauth-required`-adjacent treatment I already asked for on the facilitator's own banner is the same register I'd want here, just pointed the other direction (participant-facing, informational, not actionable). I'd frame this as: worth doing, low cost, separable from the close-code fix, and should be named as a real scope decision in Propose rather than left as "might be unnecessary."

## 3. The "reconnect and land back mid-topic" half is the one thing I actually can't evaluate without seeing it work

Devon's Open Question 1 — whether OR-1.7's full state-restore is real today or only spec'd — is the single thing I'd stop everything to get answered before I'd sign off on scope, and I want to add the facilitator-specific reason it matters more for me than for a participant: if a participant reconnects into a slightly wrong state, they lose a few seconds of confusion. If *I* reconnect and land somewhere that isn't exactly where I left off — wrong topic, readiness grid not reflecting the votes that piled up while I was gone, or (worse) landing on a generic post-login page instead of back inside the session at all — I don't have a fallback. There's no "ask the facilitator" step above me in the room. I would need to explicitly confirm, before this ships to a live team, that clicking "Log in again" from mid-session actually returns me to that same session at that same topic, readiness grid intact — not just that the backend snapshot mechanism exists, but that I can watch it happen. This is exactly the usability-testing commitment I already made for the reauth banner itself; I'd extend that same offer to this specific path (absolute-timeout mid-topic → reauth → rejoin) before any team hits it for real, and I'd want it named as a gate in tasks.md the way the banner's mock sign-off was, not assumed from a code read.

## 4. The button's destination is a real-navigation question, not just a state-machine one

`ReauthRequiredTreatment.tsx`'s CTA does `window.location.href = "/auth/login"` — a full page navigation away from the SPA. I looked for a return-to/deep-link mechanism in the OIDC client and login route and didn't find one. If there isn't one, "Log in again" today would send me to a generic post-login landing page, not back into the session I was just running — which would make Open Question 1 moot in the worst way (the state-restore mechanism could be flawless and I'd still never reach it because nothing routed me back to the session URL). I'd want Design to confirm explicitly whether this exists or needs to be added, rather than treating the close-code fix as sufficient on its own.

## 5. Does this land during a reveal the same way I already flagged for SEC-26?

I raised, in my review of the original `reauth-required` banner, that nobody had considered what happens if the signal arrives in the few seconds around a reveal. The absolute-lifetime timer makes this more likely to matter here, not less — a 90-minute session-wide clock has no reason to avoid the reveal window, and a facilitator is by definition present and active (mid-topic, about to reveal) more often than idle when their own 90 minutes runs out. I'd want this named again in Propose for this specific case, even if the answer is again "acceptable, no special handling."

## Bottom line

The close-code fix is right and I want it shipped. But three things need to be resolved — not deferred — before I'd call this done from the room's point of view: (1) the shared banner's copy can't tell a facilitator her non-existent unsubmitted vote is at risk while staying silent about what's actually true for her; (2) participants need *something*, even one quiet line, while I'm gone, because "the room stalls" is already true the moment my timer fires and today's spec has never answered what that should look like; (3) I need to see the reconnect-and-rejoin path actually work, live, mid-topic, before a real team hits it — this is the same bar I've held every other piece of this ritual to, and I don't think an absolute-timeout mid-session logout is exempt from it just because the fix looks small on the wire.
