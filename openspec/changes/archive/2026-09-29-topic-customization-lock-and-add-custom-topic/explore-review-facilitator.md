# Facilitator Review: Topic Customization Lock + Add Custom Topic (#49 + #50)

**Reviewer (persona):** Priya Nair, Facilitator (SME)
**Reviewing:** `exploration-notes.md`
**Scope reminder:** backend-only bundle, no frontend form ships here. I'm reviewing whether the groundwork being laid will let a future UI give me a good experience, not asking for UI in this bundle.

---

## Where this sits relative to what I care about most

This bundle doesn't touch the reveal, the readiness grid, or outlier flagging — my top three concerns are not at risk here. It lives entirely in the space between sessions: topic configuration. But it's squarely inside two things I *do* care about — **first-session support** ("longer and more guided by design, not by workaround") and **continuity across facilitators**. I'm reviewing it through that lens, not the live-session lens.

Overall: the exploration is thorough on data integrity, authorization philosophy, and status codes. It is noticeably thinner on what happens on my screen and in my head at the moment I hit the lock. That's the gap I want to flag.

---

## Observations

1. **The exploration correctly separates the "moment of confusion" into two very different situations, but doesn't say so explicitly, and that distinction matters.** There's the situation where I *navigate to* topic management for a new team and the UI shows me a read-only view with an explanation (the normal path, driven by `TOPIC-001`/`GET /teams/:teamId`'s `isCustomizationLocked`). And there's the situation where a write actually gets *rejected* (`TOPIC-003`'s 403/409, whichever it ends up being) — which, if the frontend correctly hides add/remove/reorder controls when locked, should almost never happen through the UI. It's the defense-in-depth path: stale tab, cache lag, a UI bug, or someone hitting the API directly. The exploration's Section 5 spends real effort on the *rejection's* status code, but my actual moment of confusion — "wait, why can't I add a topic to this team?" — happens at the *read/view* layer, not the write-rejection layer. Getting the read-side explanation right matters more to my day-to-day experience than whether the write's error is 403 or 409.

2. **Open question #4 in the exploration ("does `TOPIC-001`'s shipped response already return `isCustomizationLocked`?") is, for me, the single most important item on that list** — more important than the status-code question ranked above it. If the read endpoint doesn't yet expose that boolean, there is no way for a future frontend to show me the calm, expected "not yet — here's why" screen the use case's AC promises. Without it, the *only* way I'd discover the lock is by trying to act and getting rejected — which turns a designed, informative state into a surprise error. I'd want this nailed down before, not during, the eventual frontend work, since it changes whether #55 is building against a contract that already tells the truth or one that's silent about it.

3. **The rejection error body itself is underspecified, and this is the part of the task I was specifically asked to weigh in on.** `TOPIC-003`'s existing contract folds three unrelated causes — not a facilitator, facilitator is a team member, customization lock is active — into a single `403 Forbidden` with (as documented) no distinguishing field in the response body. The exploration's Section 5 debates 403 vs. 409 for the *status code*, but never asks whether the **body** carries a machine-readable reason (`code: "CUSTOMIZATION_LOCKED"` vs. `code: "NOT_ELIGIBLE_FACILITATOR"`) alongside a human-readable message. Status code alone can't drive the AC's "clear, plain-language explanation" — a frontend can't turn a bare 403 into three different sentences. This is exactly the "opaque error" risk the task asked me to check for, and right now the exploration doesn't resolve it, it just resolves the wrapper around it.

4. **The explanation's *content* isn't discussed at all, only that a message must exist.** The use case AC requires "a clear, plain-language explanation... stating that customization is available after the first session complete" — but neither the use case nor the exploration says whether that explanation should also tell me *why* (so it doesn't read as an arbitrary restriction) or give me a next action (e.g., pointing at running/completing the first session). For a facilitator onboarding a brand-new team, "you can't do this yet" and "you can't do this yet, because the first session needs to run against the untouched defaults so it can serve as your baseline" land very differently. The second builds trust in the tool; the first reads like a permissions wall. I'd want the proposal to at least reserve a field for an explanation string driven by server-known state (not hardcoded frontend copy that can drift from the actual rule), even though the copy itself is #55's job.

5. **Team-list-level visibility of lock state is missing from the notes.** I rotate across three teams. `TEAM-002` (`GET /teams/facilitatable`) — my landing view — returns `lastSessionDate: string | null` per team but, per the contract as documented, not `isCustomizationLocked`. `isCustomizationLocked` only shows up on `TEAM-003` (single-team detail) and `TOPIC-001`. That means today's contract makes me click into each team individually to learn whether it's still on defaults. For someone managing a rotating roster, a `null` `lastSessionDate` is a reasonable proxy ("no completed session = definitely locked"), but it's an inference the frontend has to make, not a fact the API states. Worth flagging so whoever writes the proposal decides whether `TEAM-002` should also carry the flag, or whether "infer it from `lastSessionDate === null`" is accepted as the intentional shortcut.

6. **No mechanism for me to know *who* added a custom topic, and this is a continuity issue, not a nice-to-have.** The exploration's Section 4 (Philosophy 1 vs. 2) is the right debate to have, and I don't have a strong opinion on which authorization model wins — but it surfaces a real consequence I want named regardless of which one is chosen: under Philosophy 1 (standing global facilitator + non-member), a facilitator who has never run a session for Team X can add a topic to their configuration. I checked the `topics` table (`packages/backend/migrations/2_create_tables.sql`, lines 45-59) — there's no `created_by`/`added_by_facilitator_id` column, and `TOPIC-003`'s response shape doesn't return one either. If I come back to a team after an absence, or hand off to another facilitator, and a topic has appeared that neither of us added, there's currently no way to answer "who added this and when" other than `created_at` — which tells me *when*, not *who*. That's precisely the kind of gap my "Continuity Across Facilitators" concern exists to catch. I'm not saying this bundle must solve it, but the exploration doesn't mention it at all, and if Philosophy 1 is the chosen model, the case for capturing the adding facilitator's identity gets stronger, not weaker.

7. **Audit-logging the rejected attempts (Section 7) is good and I support it, with one addition: it should be legible to me, not just to whoever reads the audit table.** The exploration frames this purely as a security/integrity measure ("did anyone try to jump the gun"). It's also directly useful to a facilitator handing off a team — if I return to a team after months away and something seems off about when customization actually unlocked, a rejected-attempt log plus the eventual successful add is the kind of history that belongs in the same continuity story as session history and trend data. I'd want it treated as facilitator-relevant record, not just an internal safety net, even if surfacing it in a UI is out of scope for this bundle.

## Questions

1. Will `TOPIC-001` (and/or `TEAM-003`) be updated as part of *this* bundle to guarantee `isCustomizationLocked` is present, or is that being deferred and re-derived later by whoever builds #55's frontend? The exploration flags this as open (item #4 in its own closing list) — I want it resolved here, not carried forward as a surprise for the frontend work.
2. Will the rejection response for a locked write include a distinguishable reason (a code or equivalent), or only a generic 403/409 with a static message? If it's the latter, how does the eventual frontend tell "you're not eligible to facilitate this team" apart from "this team's topics are locked" without a second round-trip or guesswork?
3. Is the explanatory message's content going to be driven by server-supplied text/state, or written once as static frontend copy? If the latter, what happens if the lock's underlying rule gains nuance later (e.g., a future exception case) — does the frontend copy silently go stale?
4. Independent of which authorization philosophy is chosen in Section 4: should the record of who added a custom topic be captured now (even if not displayed until a later bundle), given it's markedly cheaper to add the column at write-endpoint-creation time than to retrofit it once historical rows already lack it?
5. Should `TEAM-002` (my team-list landing view) carry `isCustomizationLocked` directly, or is "infer from `lastSessionDate === null`" accepted as the intended shortcut? If the latter, I'd like that stated as a deliberate choice rather than left to be noticed later.

---

## Suggested additions to the eventual proposal

1. **Decide and record the shape of the lock-rejection error body**, not just its status code — at minimum a stable reason code distinct from the other 403 causes on `TOPIC-003`, so a future frontend can render the correct explanation without inferring it from a bare status.
2. **Resolve the `TOPIC-001`/read-side exposure of `isCustomizationLocked` explicitly as part of this bundle's scope**, since the read-side view is where I'll actually experience the lock most of the time — not the rejected-write path.
3. **Add a line item (even if just a "confirmed as out of scope" note, matching the pattern this exploration already uses elsewhere)** on whether the identity of the facilitator who added a custom topic is captured. If it's explicitly deferred, say so on the record the way Section 8's real-time-unlock waive-off is — don't let it be silently absent.
4. **State plainly whether `TEAM-002` needs the lock flag or whether the null-session inference is the intended design**, so it isn't rediscovered as a gap once a facilitator-team-list UI is built.
5. **When the explanation-message content is eventually written (in #55 or wherever the frontend lands), make sure it says *why*, not just *that*** — a plain-language reason tied to baseline integrity will land better than an unexplained restriction, especially for a facilitator onboarding their very first new team.

None of the above are integrity-critical the way the reveal or the no-manager rule are — I don't think this bundle risks the ritual itself. But it does sit inside "first-session support" and "continuity across facilitators," two things I explicitly said the application needs to get right, and right now the exploration's attention is almost entirely on the server-side correctness of the lock and not on what happens on my screen when I meet it.
