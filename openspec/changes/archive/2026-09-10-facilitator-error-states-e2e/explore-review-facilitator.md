## Where Devon is right, and why it matters to me specifically

Devon's central finding — that zero of the four error states have any client-side rendering today, and that my sign-off on this is a case of mistaken identity — is exactly the kind of thing I asked the team to be paranoid about, and I'm glad someone was. I want to be precise about *why* this matters to me beyond "process correctness," because the reasons are workflow reasons, not paperwork reasons.

I did sign off on three strings in Group 6 of the staleness-signal work: `unknown-reconnecting`, `reauth-required`, and the grid-marker tooltip. I remember that review. Those are strings about *my own device's* connection to the session — they tell me something is wrong with my link to the room, not something wrong with the room itself. I have never seen, and was never asked to react to, in-layout copy for: a failed reveal, a "your historical data isn't available right now," a session-status-transition banner, or a cross-team denial message. Devon is correct that these are different sentences serving a different purpose, and correct that nobody should check that box on my behalf because the words "facilitator" and "banner" appear in both efforts. I'd have caught this myself in a later review, but I'd rather it get caught now, before a test locks in unreviewed strings as the source of truth.

So: **yes, I agree the four Group-10 strings need my real review before any UI ships.** Not as a formality — because I read facilitator-facing copy for tone as much as content, and these four states sit in the most sensitive place in the whole product for tone: mid-session, in front of a room, when something has already gone slightly wrong.

## What each of the four states actually feels like mid-session (this is the part I want on record)

Devon's inventory is accurate on what exists in code. Here's what's missing from a pure engineering read — how each of these lands on a facilitator who is standing in front of a room, mid-topic, with eight people watching:

**Reveal failure (recoverable, 503).** This is the single worst moment for anything to go sideways, because the reveal is the moment I care about more than any other feature in this application — I've said as much everywhere. If I hit "reveal" and get an error, I need three things in under two seconds: (1) unambiguous confirmation that *nobody else saw a partial or early reveal* — silence on this point will make me assume the worst and I will say something awkward out loud to cover for the app, (2) a retry that doesn't require me to re-explain to the room what just happened, and (3) wording that doesn't sound alarming to anyone glancing at my screen from across the table. "Try again" as Devon's notes describe it is fine as a button label, but I want to know what the *screen* says while I'm looking at it, because a raw error state here reads to me — and would read to the room if they caught a glimpse — as "the vote broke," which is the one thing I've told this team I cannot have happen even in appearance.

**Reveal failure (non-recoverable, 409 — session no longer active).** Different animal. This is recoverable *by me*, not by retrying — it means the session state moved out from under me, possibly because I fat-fingered something, or because of a stale tab from a prior session. "Review the session status" is the right instinct but it needs to tell me *what* to review and *where*, in one glance. I will not have time to hunt through a menu while eight people wait.

**Historical data unavailable during an active session (200, empty state, "still active" language).** I want to underline something Devon got exactly right and I want it protected through implementation: this must never read as an access problem. If I'm mid-session and I glance at a team's history to ground a comment I'm about to make, and I get anything that smells like "you don't have permission," I will assume either I've been de-authorized mid-session (alarming) or the app is broken (also alarming) — either way I stop trusting the screen for the rest of that session, which defeats the entire "control surface that keeps me present" goal I've described as core to this project. The distinction between "temporarily can't show you this" and "you can't see this" has to be *visually* obvious, not just correct in the response body. A grey empty-state box reads differently than a lock icon. Whoever designs this needs to know which one I'm getting, and I want to see it before it ships.

**Session status transition banner (non-blocking, "Resume or review").** I want to flag something not yet in Devon's notes: "non-blocking" is the right call architecturally, but I need to know it won't be non-blocking in a way that means *invisible*. A banner I have to notice on my own, while running a topic, is a banner I will miss half the time. I don't want a modal — modals interrupt the room — but I do want confidence that this sits somewhere in my eyeline that doesn't compete with the readiness grid I'm already watching. This is exactly the "styling decision vs. UX architecture decision" trap I warned about generally; it applies specifically here.

**Cross-team denial ("This data is not available in your current session").** From a pure facilitator-experience standpoint this is the lowest-stakes of the four, because it's the one state I'd least expect to hit accidentally in a normal session — it implies I've navigated somewhere I shouldn't be pointed in the first place. My only note: the non-disclosure property Devon confirms (no team ID, name, or existence leak) is right, and I'd add that the *tone* shouldn't imply I did something wrong. I may have gotten there via a stale link or a bookmark from a prior rotation, not by trying to snoop on another team's data.

## Does the split Devon proposes get us to "disappears into the background"?

Split into two pieces, with piece 2 gated on my real sign-off — yes, conditionally. My worry about deferred UI work is never "it's slower," it's "it ships anyway, quietly, without me." Devon's notes explicitly reject that shortcut and name a real owner and a real gate for piece 2, which is the thing that makes deferral safe rather than risky. If piece 2 is tracked the way Group 6 was tracked — a visible, open item with my name on the review step — then no, deferring it does not leave facilitators exposed, because nothing ships to a live session in the meantime. The two connection-health strings that exist today already work exactly as reviewed. My concern would only trigger if someone later reads "backend/WebSocket piece done" and infers the feature is done — which is the exact drift Devon is trying to prevent by insisting on the split being explicit in the archive.

One gap I'd add to piece 1, so it doesn't get lost: Devon flags that `FacilitatorConnectionHost` has no client-side facilitator-role check yet, and that this is harmless only while the component renders stub data. I want that checklist item explicitly inherited by whichever change wires in real historical data or reveal controls — not filed away as a comment in `App.tsx` that a future engineer has to happen to notice. A facilitator-only screen that quietly stops checking who the facilitator is, the same day it starts showing real cross-team-sensitive content, is precisely the kind of "styling decision instead of architecture decision" failure mode I keep raising.

## Usability items I want captured before piece 2's proposal gets written (not before piece 1 — piece 1 doesn't touch UI)

1. **A visual language for "data hiccup" vs. "access problem," decided once and reused across states 1, 2, and 3** — not decided independently per screen. If reveal failure, historical-data-unavailable, and the transition banner each invent their own visual grammar for "something's temporarily off," I'll have three things to learn instead of one, and so will every other facilitator I hand a team to.
2. **Reveal failure needs a spec for what the *other participants* see, not just what I see**, even though the failure is facilitator-facing. If my screen errors, I need to know with certainty whether their screens show "waiting for reveal" or nothing changed at all — this is the simultaneity guarantee extended to the failure path, and it hasn't been named as in-scope anywhere in what I've read.
3. **Placement of the session-transition banner relative to the readiness grid**, specified as layout, not left to whoever builds it. I've asked for this generally; I'm asking again specifically for this banner because it's the one most likely to appear while I'm mid-topic and least likely to be looked for.
4. **A "what do I do next" phrase for all four**, worded for someone mid-facilitation, not worded for a support ticket. I'd like to review these in the actual layout, per my usual ask, not as a list of strings in a spec doc — copy that reads fine in isolation can still read wrong next to a live readiness grid with real names on it.

Split the work, keep piece 2 visibly open with my name on the review step, and bring me the four strings in situ — same as Group 6 — before any of this reaches a live session.
