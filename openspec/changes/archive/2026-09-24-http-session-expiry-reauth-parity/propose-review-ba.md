# BA Review: `http-session-expiry-reauth-parity` proposal

Reviewer: Marcus Delgado (Business Analyst)
Scope of review: proposal.md, design.md, tasks.md, specs/http-session-expiry-signal/spec.md, specs/reauth-return-to/spec.md — checked against BRD.md NFR-AUTH-005/SEC-26 and against the actual call sites in the codebase (I read `packages/backend/src/routes/teams.ts`, `packages/frontend/src/components/MemberManagement.tsx`, `packages/frontend/src/pages/SessionLobbyPage.tsx`, `packages/frontend/src/auth/middleware.ts`, `packages/frontend/src/components/ReauthRequiredTreatment.tsx`, and `packages/shared/src/types/auth.ts` directly rather than taking the proposal's inventory on faith).

## Bottom line

The capabilities are mostly specific and buildable — Decision 1's helper contract, Decision 3's regex-per-shape style, and Decision 6's inline-rendering rule are all precise enough to build from without a round trip back to me. But I found one finding I'd block on (Finding 1 — a missed call site that directly contradicts the stated deferral reasoning) and one finding I'd want resolved before implementation starts on `SessionLobbyPage.tsx` (Finding 2 — an implicit acceptance criterion that, read literally, is impossible to satisfy for one of the three call sites). Neither is a rewrite; both are scoped, answerable questions.

---

## Finding 1 (blocking): `MemberManagement.tsx` has a second call site the scope decision never addresses, and it defeats the stated deferral reasoning

Proposal.md's scope decision defers `MemberManagement.tsx` on two stated grounds:

> "it's a team-membership roster (`GET /teams/:teamId`)... with no ritual action and no facilitator-triggered POST that could 401 mid-something-irreversible... its one call site currently reads `teams.ts`'s `category: "invalid_request"` label for a narrow 'user row missing' edge case, not the `session_expired` category this change's detection keys on."

Both clauses describe only the `GET /api/v1/teams/:teamId` call (`MemberManagement.tsx:101`, `loadMembers`). That's accurate for that call site. But `MemberManagement.tsx` has a **second** fetch call site the proposal doesn't mention: `submitRoleChange` (`MemberManagement.tsx:127`), a `PATCH /api/v1/teams/:teamId/members/:userId/role` — the role-change action, gated on EM/Application-Admin authorization (`checkAssignRolesAuthorization`, `teams.ts:737`), with its own two-step confirm flow (422 `requiresConfirmation` → re-submit with `confirmedZeroParticipant: true`, `teams.ts:702-706`).

Checking this against the actual backend: `authMiddleware`'s `onRequest` hook is registered globally (`app.ts:101`), and `/api/v1/teams/:teamId/members/:userId/role` is not in `PUBLIC_ROUTES` (`middleware.ts:10-24`). So a session that hits the 90-minute absolute lifetime mid-`submitRoleChange` gets exactly the same `category: "session_expired"` 401 this change is built to detect — not the mislabeled `invalid_request` category the proposal cites as MemberManagement.tsx's reason for exclusion. That mislabeling is real, but it's specific to the `GET` call site (per exploration's Correction 1); it does not extend to this `PATCH`.

Today, `submitRoleChange`'s `!res.ok` branch (`MemberManagement.tsx:154-160`) falls into the same generic handling every other in-scope call site had before this change: `body.error?.message ?? "Role change failed."` — a message that, on a session-expiry, tells an EM or Admin to retry an action that cannot succeed, with no indication their session is gone. This is precisely the failure mode proposal.md's "Why" section describes as the problem this change exists to fix, on a call site not covered by either of the two grounds the proposal gives for deferring this component.

I checked this isn't a stretch by reading NFR-AUTH-005 and SEC-26 in BRD.md directly: the 90-minute absolute lifetime is stated as a property of the session, not of any particular ritual flow. Nothing in the requirements scopes it to voting/session-facilitation actions only. A role-change action gated on an authorization capability, mutating state, with a confirm step in front of it, is not meaningfully different in kind from `DraftSessionHost.tsx`'s `advance` — the call site design.md Decision 4 spends a full paragraph on specifically because of its confirm-step-plus-irreversible-action shape.

**What I need resolved, one of:**
1. Pull `submitRoleChange` into this change's scope alongside `SessionCreationPage.tsx` — it's gated on the same class of "does this user have an elevated capability" check, and the proposal's own logic for including `SessionCreationPage.tsx` ("the same facilitator audience... an inconsistent, confusing half-measure" to fix one screen and not the adjacent one) applies here with EM/Admin substituted for facilitator. This is a sixth call site, not a new page — the shared helper this change builds handles it with the same shape of change as the other five.
2. Or, if there's a reason to defer it anyway (team capacity, different reviewer, whatever), the deferral needs its own stated justification — not inherited from the `GET` call site's justification, which doesn't apply. Update proposal.md's scope-decision section to address `submitRoleChange` explicitly, on its own terms, the same way `SessionCreationPage.tsx` got its own paragraph instead of being silently bundled with `DraftSessionHost.tsx`'s reasoning.

Either answer is fine by me. What I'd flag as a real gap is the current state: the deferral reads as a decision about "`MemberManagement.tsx`" as a whole, but the reasoning given only accounts for one of its two call sites. That's exactly the kind of inconsistent in/out line I was asked to check for.

## Finding 2 (should resolve before implementation, not blocking the proposal itself): `role` is not determinable for `SessionLobbyPage.tsx`'s `action-items-review` GET at the moment it needs to be

Spec.md states the role rule for `SessionLobbyPage.tsx`'s three call sites as one clause: *"`role="participant"` unless the caller is the session's facilitator, in which case `role="facilitator"`, matching this page's existing facilitator/participant distinction."* Design.md doesn't add anything more specific per call site.

That's implementable for two of the three: `start` and `begin-voting` are only reachable from UI gated on `branch.isFacilitator` already being `true` (`SessionLobbyPage.tsx:234`, the facilitator-only controls block) — by the time either POST fires, the page already knows the caller is the facilitator.

It is **not** implementable as written for the third: `action-items-review` (`fetchReview`, `SessionLobbyPage.tsx:74`). Reading the code directly — this is the call that *establishes* `isFacilitator` in the first place (`setBranch({ kind: "lobby", isFacilitator: body.isFacilitator })`, line 87, only reached on a 200 or a 409). If this exact call returns a 401 session-expiry instead, `isFacilitator` was never set for this page instance — "this page's existing facilitator/participant distinction" the spec says to match doesn't exist yet at that point. There's no fallback stated.

This isn't cosmetic. `ReauthRequiredTreatment`'s `role` prop controls whether the vote-loss sentence renders (`ReauthRequiredTreatment.tsx:136-140`, `"Any vote you haven't submitted yet will be lost."`). Facilitators never vote — the component's own comment says the sentence is "factually inapplicable" to that role. Guessing wrong here isn't a missing nice-to-have; it either tells a facilitator they're about to lose a vote they were never going to cast, or omits a true warning to a participant genuinely about to lose one.

Tasks.md task 3.1 and 3.6 both say "the appropriate role" / "the correct role" for this call site without saying how it's computed. I checked whether there's an existing signal available before this fetch resolves that could substitute: `useAuth()`'s `session` exposes `canFacilitateSessions` (`packages/shared/src/types/auth.ts:28`) — a general capability flag, already in scope at `SessionLobbyPage.tsx:63` (`const { session } = useAuth();`) and not yet used elsewhere on this page. It's not a perfect proxy — it answers "can this user facilitate sessions at all," not "is this user the facilitator of *this* session" — but it may be the only signal actually available at that point, and it's likely right in practice (only one facilitator per team, and the distinction only changes which sentence renders, not any authorization decision).

**What I need:** design.md Decision 1 or 2 should state explicitly how `role` is determined for the `action-items-review` call site specifically — either "use `session?.canFacilitateSessions` as the role signal for this call site only" (and say why that's an acceptable proxy given the only consequence is sentence inclusion, not authorization) or some other explicit rule. Whatever it is, tasks.md 3.1/3.6 should name it instead of "the appropriate role," so nobody has to make this call unilaterally mid-implementation.

## Everything else: specific enough to build from

Checking the rest of the capabilities against "would an implementer have to come back to me":

- **Decision 1 (shared helper contract).** Precise: exact status+category check stated twice (design.md and spec.md, word-for-word consistent), the "owns the single `.json()` read, callers don't pre-parse" rule is explicit and the *reason* is given concretely (DraftSessionHost's existing double-read hazard), and the three helper-level test scenarios in spec.md (recognized / wrong category / non-401) are unambiguous and directly portable to unit tests. No notes.
- **Decision 3 (`returnTo` allow-list additions).** The `/team/:teamId/session/:sessionId` shape is fully specified (regex given verbatim, UUID-pinned both segments, matches existing style). The `/sessions/new` literal-path shape is also fully specified, and — importantly, since this is a genuinely new shape class for this allow-list, as design.md itself flags — the spec.md scenarios include both a positive case and an explicit near-miss rejection case (`/sessions/new/anything`, `/sessions/newer`, spec.md lines 39-43), and tasks.md 2.3 mirrors both. That's the right level of scrutiny for a "first non-UUID-anchored entry" — I don't think this needs tightening. One small gap, not worth blocking on: neither the spec scenarios nor tasks.md 2.3 explicitly exercises `/sessions/new?foo=bar` (the "optional trailing query string tolerated" clause in design.md Decision 3) as an *accepted* case — only the literal path and the near-misses are tested. Worth adding one line to tasks.md 2.3 so the query-string tolerance is actually verified, not just asserted in prose.
- **Decision 2 (dual-signal / idempotency on `SessionLobbyPage.tsx`).** The "first-wins/second-is-no-op" rule is stated as a single boolean gate, not a priority order, and spec.md gives both orderings as explicit scenarios (lines 57-67) plus the "neither fired" baseline (69-72). Tasks.md 3.8 tests both orderings. This is exactly the kind of thing that's easy to leave ambiguous ("whichever is more important wins") and wasn't — good.
- **Decision 4/5 (no-partial-execution, returnTo-lands-on-page-not-mid-action).** Both are stated as explicit, separate acceptance conditions in spec.md (the GET and POST scenarios for `DraftSessionHost.tsx` are two distinct scenarios, not one implying the other, matching Priya's exploration-review catch that a mount-time GET fix is easy to skip). Decision 4's "named limitation, not fixed here" about the `confirming` phase not surviving reauth is stated as a locked-in behavior with its own test (tasks.md 4.6), not left as an ambiguous edge case — good, this is exactly the kind of thing that becomes a scope dispute later if it's silently assumed instead of written down.
- **Decision 6 (inline rendering, `DraftSessionHost.tsx` visual collision with the confirm dialog).** Correctly flagged as needing human visual review rather than assumed away — tasks.md 4.7 requires a mock/screenshot review before the page's visual treatment is "settled." That's the right call given this is the one page where the reauth banner renders into the same layout slot as an existing two-button confirm dialog under real time pressure. I'd add one acceptance condition tasks.md 4.7 doesn't currently state explicitly: the mock review should confirm the CTA is distinguishable *by more than color alone* (e.g., label text, position, or shape) given the confirm dialog's own buttons and the reauth CTA are all rendered as plain buttons — a facilitator scanning quickly under time pressure is the stated risk, and color-only differentiation is a common way that kind of review still ends up ambiguous in practice.

## Cross-check against requirements/

NFR-AUTH-005 and SEC-26 (BRD.md) establish the 90-minute absolute lifetime and require disclosed re-authentication handling; both are already fully implemented by `session-timeout-continuity` and correctly treated by this proposal as unmodified prerequisites, not new work. I found nothing in requirements/ that this proposal's stated scope contradicts, beyond Finding 1 above (which is a completeness gap in the proposal's own stated reasoning, not a conflict with a requirement).

## Answering the two specific questions I was asked

1. **Is the `SessionCreationPage.tsx`-in / `MemberManagement.tsx`-out split well-justified and consistently applied?** The reasoning given for each is individually sound and well-argued — I have no objection to `SessionCreationPage.tsx` being in-scope (the "one screen earlier in a continuous facilitator journey" argument is concrete and correctly declines to treat the two-screen flow as separable), and the EM-pages-plus-`MemberManagement.tsx` bundle-deferral reasoning is sound for what it actually covers (read-only, no ritual action). But it is **not consistently applied**, because it doesn't cover all of `MemberManagement.tsx` — see Finding 1. The split's logic is right; its inventory is incomplete.
2. **Is the `/sessions/new` allow-list shape specified precisely enough?** Yes, with one small test-coverage gap noted above (query-string acceptance isn't exercised, only asserted in prose). The shape itself, its regex, and its near-miss rejection cases are all explicit and testable as written.
