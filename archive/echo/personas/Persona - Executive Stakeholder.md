# Persona: Executive Stakeholder

## Identity

**Name:** Patricia Chen
**Title:** VP of Engineering
**Reports To:** Chief Technology Officer
**Scope:** Oversees all engineering teams across the organization (12 teams, ~150 engineers)

---

## Background

Patricia Chen has spent 18 years in software engineering, rising from individual contributor to VP of Engineering at a mid-size technology company. She started as a backend engineer, led teams at multiple startups, and most recently joined the current organization three years ago to scale the engineering function from 40 to 150+ engineers.

Patricia was introduced to Engineering Health Checks during her tenure at a previous company where the practice was instrumental in turning around a struggling engineering culture. When she joined her current organization, she found no systematic way to measure or discuss team health—decisions were made based on gut feeling and anecdotal evidence. She championed the adoption of Health Checks across all teams and has been a driving force behind the initiative.

As VP, Patricia doesn't attend live sessions (to maintain psychological safety), but she receives detailed reports after each session and reviews trends quarterly. She sees this web application as critical infrastructure that will scale the Health Check practice beyond what manual tools can support.

---

## Motivations

### Scaling Engineering Health Across the Organization

Patricia wants to implement consistent, organization-wide health measurements that allow her to identify struggling teams early and allocate resources strategically. The current manual process (spreadsheets, ad-hoc surveys) doesn't scale and lacks the rigor needed for data-driven leadership. She needs trend visualization and historical data to spot patterns across her 12 teams.

### Data-Driven Resource Allocation

As a VP, Patricia must make the case for engineering investments to the CTO and CFO. She needs quantifiable data on team health to justify headcount requests, tooling investments, and process changes. The application should provide aggregate views that help her make the business case for interventions.

### Building a Culture of Continuous Improvement

Patricia believes that engineering health directly impacts product quality, delivery speed, and employee retention. She wants the Health Check process to be a catalyst for positive cultural change—not a blame exercise. The application must support this by making discussions constructive and action-oriented.

### Reducing Manager Overhead

Her Engineering Managers spend too much time on manual Health Check administration—collecting votes, calculating averages, and compiling reports. Patricia wants the application to automate these tasks so managers can focus on coaching and helping their teams improve.

---

## Relationship to the Project

Patricia is the executive sponsor for this project. She approved the budget and has made it a priority for the engineering organization. While she is not involved in day-to-day decisions, she receives bi-weekly updates and signs off on major milestones.

She cares most about:
- **Trend visualization and reporting:** Needs aggregate views across teams to identify patterns and justify investments
- **Action item tracking:** Wants visibility into what teams are committing to improve and whether they're following through
- **Data integrity and security:** Ensures sensitive health data is properly protected and only accessible to appropriate roles
- **Scalability:** Needs the application to handle 12+ teams without degradation

She explicitly defers to Engineering Managers on facilitation best practices and to facilitators on session flow decisions.

---

## Success Criteria

Patricia will consider this project successful when:

1. **Adoption is widespread.** All 12 engineering teams are conducting regular Health Checks using the application within 6 months of launch.

2. **Data is actionable.** She can generate a quarterly report showing health trends across teams, with clear identification of teams needing intervention and teams showing improvement.

3. **Manager time is reduced.** Engineering Managers report that session administration takes 50% less time than the current manual process.

4. **Correlation to business outcomes.** She can demonstrate a positive correlation between Health Check scores and key metrics like delivery velocity, incident rates, and retention.

---

## Concerns and Risks

- **Adoption fatigue.** Teams may resist yet another tool, especially if the Health Check process is perceived as punitive. She needs the team to emphasize psychological safety and continuous improvement over measurement for its own sake.

- **Data misuse.** There is risk that health data could be used inappropriately—to blame teams or justify premature restructuring. She needs clear guidelines on how data can and cannot be used, built into the application's access controls.

- **Investment vs. value.** If the application doesn't provide clear ROI compared to the current manual process, she may face pressure to deprioritize it. The reporting and trend features must deliver tangible value.

- **Facilitator availability.** The current facilitator model requires trained facilitators for each session. If facilitators burn out or leave, the process could stall. She wants the application to make facilitation easier, not harder.
