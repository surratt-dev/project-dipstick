## Vote Types

### Finger Voting

Each participant holds up 1, 2, 3, or 4 fingers to communicate their response. 1 is poor; 4 is very good. In practice, the facilitator communicates this simply as "1 is bad, 4 is good" — there is no need to define every point on the scale.

This vote type is used when a topic is likely to produce a range of opinions across the team. The graduated scale allows individuals to express degree of satisfaction, not just direction, which makes trends more meaningful over time.

An even number of choices is deliberate. With no middle value to default to, each participant must commit to a position that is either "more good than bad" or "more bad than good." This keeps the results honest and prevents the team from clustering in the center to avoid conflict.

| Fingers | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Poor — significant problems or frustration |
| 2 | Below average — more bad than good |
| 3 | Above average — more good than bad |
| 4 | Very good — working well |

---

### Roman Vote

A thumbs up or thumbs down. Up is good; down is bad.

This vote type is used when the topic being evaluated is essentially binary — either the thing is working or it isn't. A range of opinion is not meaningful here. For example, asking whether tests are consistent is a pass/fail question: either the suite is reliable or it isn't. Allowing degrees of response would obscure what should be a clear signal.

| Gesture | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Thumbs up | Good — the condition is met |
| Thumbs down | Bad — the condition is not met |

---

### Modified Roman

A thumbs up, thumbs down, or flat open hand held palm-down.

This is a variant of the Roman vote used exclusively for evaluating trends. The addition of the neutral gesture acknowledges that some teams are neither improving nor declining — they are holding steady — and that this is a legitimate state to capture.

The flat open hand is the standard gesture for a neutral/steady vote. It is visually distinct from both a thumb up and a thumb down, making it easy to read across the room at a glance.

| Gesture | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Thumbs up | Trending positive — things are improving |
| Flat open hand (palm down) | Steady — no significant change |
| Thumbs down | Trending negative — things are getting worse |

---

## Voting Process

Before each vote, the facilitator states the prompt, the vote type being used, and briefly recaps what the gestures mean for that topic. This ensures everyone is interpreting the vote the same way before they commit.

Participants take a few seconds to consider the topic, then extend a closed fist to signal they are ready. Once everyone has a closed fist, the facilitator counts down — "3, 2, 1, vote" — and all participants reveal their vote simultaneously.

Simultaneous reveal is important. It prevents participants from anchoring on each other's responses before they have committed to their own position.
